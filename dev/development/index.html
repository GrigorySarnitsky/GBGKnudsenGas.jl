Estimators for means and their standard errors

When studying the flow of a Knudsen gas past a body, it is natural to collect statistics only from gas particles that actually hit the body, since particles that miss it neither change their state nor affect the body. In a Monte Carlo simulation, this corresponds to collecting data based on a fixed, predetermined number $N_\text{hit}$ of collided particles (and stopping once we reach $N_\text{hit}$). If $N_\text{miss}$ denotes the number of particles that missed the body, then the total number of simulated particles is $N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit}$. Unlike $N_\text{hit}$, $N_\text{miss}$ is a random variable, and consequently so is the total number of simulated particles. This randomness requires care when computing statistics across all of the particles.

Let $\boldsymbol F$ be a (tensor) quantity to be averaged over the particles, for example, the change in particle momentum. Crucially, for non-colliding particles, $\boldsymbol F = 0$. Our goal is to compute the average $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle$ across all of the particles, both that hit the body and that missed it. One can come up with a naive Monte Carlo estimator

\[ \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive} = \frac{1}{N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{hit}} \boldsymbol F_i,\]

with $\boldsymbol F_i$ being the value of $\boldsymbol F$ for $i$-th colliding particle. The respective naive covariance of $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive}$ (its measure of accuracy) would be

\[ \begin{align*} \operatorname{cov}(\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive}) &= \bigl< \bigl[ \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive} - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \bigr] \otimes \bigl[ \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive} - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle\bigr] \bigr> \\ &= \frac{1}{(N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit}) (N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit} - 1)} \biggl[ \sum_{i = 1}^{N_\text{hit}} \bigl[ \boldsymbol F_i - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive} \bigr] \otimes \bigl[ \boldsymbol F_i - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive} \bigr] + N_\text{miss} \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive} \biggr]. \end{align*}\]

These would be correct and unbiased estimators if both $N_\text{hit}$ and $N_\text{miss}$ were deterministic quantities. However, since $N_\text{miss}$ is random, we instead take a different approach.

Let $P(\text{hit})$ denote the probability that a particle hits the body, and let $\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle$ denote the conditional average of $\boldsymbol F$ over particles that hit the body. Then $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle$ can be written as

\[\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle = \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle P(\text{hit}).\]

Since $N_\text{hit}$ is deterministic, the unbiased Monte Carlo estimator for $\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle$ is

\[\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} = \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}} \sum_{i = 1}^{N_\text{hit}} \boldsymbol F_i.\]

In our setup, we continue the simulation until the predetermined number of collisions $N_\text{hit}$ is reached, at which point we immediately stop. This implies that $N_\text{miss}$ follows the negative binomial distribution. The corresponding unbiased Monte Carlo estimator for $P(\text{hit})$ is

\[P_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) = \frac{N_\text{hit} - 1}{N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit} - 1}.\]

Then the estimator for $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle$ can be written down as

\[\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC} = \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} P_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) = \frac{1}{N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit} - 1} \frac{N_\text{hit} - 1}{N_\text{hit}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{hit}} \boldsymbol F_i.\]

Compare this to the naive estimator $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC, naive}$.

Next, we want to quantify the accuracy of $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC}$, i.e. find its covariance. For this we first find the covariances of $\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} $ and $P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})$.

Covariances of the conditional estimators

Covariance of $\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}$

To quantify the accuracy of $\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}$, we need to calculate its covariance:

\[ \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} ) = \Bigl< \bigl[ \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \bigr] \otimes \bigl[ \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \bigr] \Bigr>.\]

It helps to remember that $\langle {\cdot} \rangle$ here denotes averaging over the ensemble of Monte Carlo simulations, each of each having exactly $N_\text{hit}$ collided particles.

To calculate $\operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} )$, we use the unbiased property of $\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}$ (its average coincides with the true average):

\[\bigl< \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \bigr> = \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle,\]

and that two measurements, $i$ and $j$, are statistically independent, leading to

\[\langle \boldsymbol F_i \otimes \boldsymbol F_j \mid \text{hit} \rangle = \langle \boldsymbol F_i \mid \text{hit} \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F_j \mid \text{hit} \rangle \quad\text{if $i \neq j$}.\]

With that, we proceed:

\[ \begin{align*} \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} ) &= \Bigl< \bigl[ \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \bigr] \otimes \bigl[ \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \bigr] \Bigr> \\ &= \bigl< \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \bigr> - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \\ &= \Bigl< \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}^2} \sum_{i = 1}^{N_\text{hit}} \sum_{j = 1}^{N_\text{hit}} \boldsymbol F_i \otimes \boldsymbol F_j \Bigr> - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \\ &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}^2} \sum_{i = 1}^{N_\text{hit}} \sum_{j = 1}^{N_\text{hit}} \langle \boldsymbol F_i \otimes \boldsymbol F_j \mid \text{hit} \rangle - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \\ &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}^2} \Bigl[ N_\text{hit} \langle \boldsymbol F \otimes \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle + N_\text{hit} (N_\text{hit} - 1) \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \Bigr] - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \\ &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}} \bigl[\langle \boldsymbol F \otimes \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \bigr]. \end{align*}\]

Thus we confirmed the usual formula for the standard error:

\[ \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}) = \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}} \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit}).\]

Using the unbiased estimator for the covariance

\[ \operatorname{cov}_\text{MC}(\boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit}) = \frac{1}{N_\text{hit} - 1} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{hit}} \bigl[ \boldsymbol F_i - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \bigr] \otimes \bigl[ \boldsymbol F_i - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \bigr],\]

we finally get

\[ \begin{align*} \operatorname{cov}_\text{MC}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} ) &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit} (N_\text{hit} - 1)} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{hit}} \bigl[ \boldsymbol F_i - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \bigr] \otimes \bigl[ \boldsymbol F_i - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \bigr] \\ &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit} (N_\text{hit} - 1)} \biggl( \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{hit}} \boldsymbol F_i \otimes \boldsymbol F_i - N_\text{hit} \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \biggr) \\ &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit} - 1} \biggl[ \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{hit}} \boldsymbol F_i \otimes \boldsymbol F_i - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}\biggr]. \end{align*}\]

Variance of $P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})$

Now we calculate the variance of $P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})$,

\[ \operatorname{var}(P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) = \bigl< \bigl[P_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) - P(\text{hit})\bigr]^2 \bigr>.\]

For this we need to use the probability mass function $f$ of $N_\text{miss}$:

\[ f(N_\text{miss}) = \frac{(N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit} - 1)!}{(N_\text{hit} - 1)! N_\text{miss}!} P^{N_\text{hit}}(\text{hit}) \bigl[1 - P(\text{hit}) \bigr]^{N_\text{miss}}.\]

We proceed with calculating $P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})$, studying in the limit of large $N_\text{hit}$:

\[ \begin{align*} \operatorname{var}(P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) &= \bigl< \bigl[P_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) - P(\text{hit})\bigr]^2 \bigr> \\ &= \sum_{N_{\text{miss}} = 0}^\infty \biggl( \frac{N_\text{hit} - 1}{N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit} - 1} - P(\text{hit})\biggr)^{\!2} \frac{(N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit} - 1)!}{(N_\text{hit} - 1)! N_\text{miss}!} P^{N_\text{hit}}(\text{hit}) \bigl[1 - P(\text{hit}) \bigr]^{N_\text{miss}} \\ &= P^2(\text{hit}) \bigl[ {}_2F_1(1, 1, N_\text{hit}, 1 - P(\text{hit})) - 1 \bigr] \\ &= P^2(\text{hit}) \biggl[ \frac{1 - P(\text{hit})}{N_\text{hit}} + \frac{2(1 - P(\text{hit}))^2}{N_\text{hit}(N_\text{hit} + 1)} + \frac{6 (1 - P(\text{hit}))^3}{N_\text{hit}(N_\text{hit} + 1)(N_\text{hit} + 2)} + \ldots \biggr], \end{align*}\]

where is ${}_2 F_1$ the hypergeometric function. The above can be checked with the following Mathematica code:

f = (m + n - 1)!/((n - 1)! m!) (1 - p)^m p^n;
pmc = (n - 1)/(m + n - 1);
s = FullSimplify[Sum[(pmc - p)^2 f, {m, 0, Infinity}]]
Sum[SeriesCoefficient[s, {(1 - p), 0, k}] (1 - p)^k, {k, 0, 4}]

with an output

p^2 (-1 + Hypergeometric2F1[1, 1, n, 1 - p])

((1 - p) p^2)/n + (2 (1 - p)^2 p^2)/(n (1 + n)) + (6 (1 - p)^3 p^2)/(n (1 + n) (2 + n)) + (24 (1 - p)^4 p^2)/(n (1 + n) (2 + n) (3 + n))

Thus to estimate the accuracy of $P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})$ we use the below biased estimator for its variance:

\[ \operatorname{var}_\text{MC} (P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) = \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}} P_\text{MC}^2(\text{hit}) \bigl[1 - P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})\bigr].\]

Covariance of the unconditional estimator $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle$

With the known covariances of $P_\text{MC}$ and $\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}$ we are well equiped for finding the covariacne of $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC} = \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})$. Since we a dealing with the product of two independent random variables, its coavariacne is

\[ \begin{align*} \operatorname{cov}(\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC}) &= \Bigl< \bigl[ \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC} - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \bigr] \otimes \bigl[ \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC} - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \bigr] \Bigr> \\ &= \operatorname{cov}(\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) \\ &= \Bigl< P^2_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \Bigr> - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \\ &= \bigl< P^2_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) \bigr> \bigl< \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \bigr> - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \\ &= \Bigl[ \operatorname{var} (P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) + P^2(\text{hit}) \Bigr] \Bigl[\operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}) + \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle\otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \Bigr] - \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle \\ &= \langle P^2_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) \rangle \operatorname{cov}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}) + \operatorname{var} (P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle. \end{align*}\]

Then we estimate $\operatorname{cov}(\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC})$ as:

\[ \operatorname{cov}_\text{MC}(\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC}) = P^2_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) \operatorname{cov}_\text{MC}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}) + \operatorname{var}_\text{MC} (P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) \, \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}.\]

Note that this is a straightforward estimator which might be biased.

Summary of the estimators

To summarize, consider a Monte Carlo simulation of a Knudsen gas flowing past a body. We run it until we find $N_\text{hit}$ particles that collided with the body and then stop. During the simulation we also count the number of particles that miss the body, $N_\text{miss}$. Let $\boldsymbol F$ be a (tensor) quantity to be averaged over the particles, for example, the change in particle momentum. Crucially, for non-colliding particles, $\boldsymbol F = 0$. We want to compute the average $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle$ across all of the particles, both those that hit the body and those that miss it. Proper Monte Carlo estimators for $\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle$ and its covariance are:

\[\boxed{ \begin{align*} \langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC} &=\langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} P_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) \\[2ex] \operatorname{cov}_\text{MC}(\langle \boldsymbol F \rangle_\text{MC}) &= P^2_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) \operatorname{cov}_\text{MC}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}) + \operatorname{var}_\text{MC} (P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) \, \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \\[1ex] \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}} \sum_{i = 1}^{N_\text{hit}} \boldsymbol F_i, \\[2ex] P_\text{MC}(\text{hit}) &= \frac{N_\text{hit} - 1}{N_\text{miss} + N_\text{hit} - 1}, \\[2ex] \operatorname{cov}_\text{MC}(\boldsymbol \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}) &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit} - 1} \biggl[ \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{hit}} \boldsymbol F_i \otimes \boldsymbol F_i - \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC} \otimes \langle \boldsymbol F \mid \text{hit} \rangle_\text{MC}\biggr], \\[2ex] \operatorname{var}_\text{MC} (P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})) &= \frac{1}{N_\text{hit}} P_\text{MC}^2(\text{hit}) \bigl[1 - P_\text{MC}(\text{hit})\bigr]. \end{align*} }\]

Extending the package

New body kinds

To add another kind of body, you define a new body type (e.g. Ellipsoid) and implement the corresponding collide_with_body method:

collide_with_body(particle::Particle, elipsoid::Ellipsoid)::Union{Nothing, Particle}

The existing implementation for Sphere can be used for reference.

Rotating bodies

Rotating bodies, in the limit of small rotational speed, can be added similarly to non-specular collisions. That's because to first order the body rotation merely modifies the collision law.

Arbitrary meshes

Currently, arbitrary meshes for bodies are not supported, but that would be a valuable feature to add.
